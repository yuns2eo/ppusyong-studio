## What is fixed: PPUSYONG's identity

### Overall silhouette

PPUSYONG is a small, stylized, anthropomorphic Bichon plush mascot. Preserve:

- a very large round head, visibly wider and taller than the compact body
- a softly flattened lower face and a full, cloud-like head silhouette
- a tiny, chubby, upright torso
- short rounded arms that hang close to the sides of the body
- short, stubby legs and two rounded feet
- a small round fluffy tail centered low on the back
- a cute plush-toy volume, never a realistic dog anatomy or a human body

The head must remain dominant: it is much larger than the body and should feel heavy, soft, and rounded.

### Fur and material

Preserve the exact material language shown in the references:

- warm ivory / creamy off-white fur, not pure paper white
- dense, tiny, evenly distributed looped curls
- soft boucle-like plush fibers with a matte finish
- rounded, padded edges and gentle fabric depth

Do not create long fur, silky strands, glossy plastic, smooth foam, visible individual animal hairs, a different fur color, or patchy markings.

### Face

Preserve:

- two tiny, narrow, sleepy dark eyes set close together
- a small dark-brown/near-black rounded nose centered below the eyes
- a very small restrained mouth beneath the nose; no teeth or tongue
- two soft peach-coral oval blush patches on the cheeks
- a small, softly protruding muzzle integrated into the lower face

The default expression is gentle, calm, and slightly sleepy. A scene may suggest subtle emotion through pose, eyelid angle, and mouth tension, but never change the eye size, eye spacing, nose shape, blush placement, or facial proportions. Do not add eyebrows, large anime eyes, eyelashes, teeth, a wide open mouth, or a human-like expression.

### Signature features

Preserve:

- one small plush curl emerging from the top center of the head, with the same scale and curl form
- two long, rounded, drooping ears attached low on each side of the head
- one small round fluffy tail centered on the lower back

These are three-dimensional features. Their visible direction may naturally change with the camera, but their anchor points, scale, and shape must not change.
